Organic farming knowledge base

Organic farming — from sowing to harvest

Guidance is built from ICAR, TNAU, AAU/JAU and other institutional agricultural material. Exact rates are shown only where a source supports the crop and context.

Important: Organic does not automatically mean risk-free. Use only correctly identified, legally permitted bio-inputs/biopesticides, follow their label where applicable, and confirm crop-specific use with a KVK/agriculture expert when the source is not crop-specific.
Step-by-step

Core organic methods

🌱

Sowing & seed preparation

  • Use clean, healthy seed suited to the local agro-climatic zone.
  • Prefer crop rotation and diversification; avoid repeating the same crop continuously.
  • Where an approved biofertilizer/biocontrol seed treatment is recommended for the crop, use the product and rate specified by the official label or institutional package of practices.
  • Prepare a fine, well-drained seedbed and avoid waterlogging.
🪱

Organic fertilizer & soil health

  • Use well-decomposed FYM, compost, vermicompost and suitable green manure to build soil organic matter.
  • Recycle crop residues safely instead of burning them.
  • Biofertilizers can supplement nutrient management, but they do not guarantee that every crop's full nutrient demand will be met.
  • Soil testing remains important even in organic systems.
🐞

Pest control without routine chemical spraying

  • Start with field scouting, clean cultivation, crop rotation, resistant varieties and balanced nutrition.
  • Use yellow/blue sticky traps, light traps or pheromone traps where appropriate for monitoring.
  • Conserve spiders, ladybird beetles, parasitoids and other natural enemies.
  • Botanicals and microbial biocontrols such as neem products, Trichoderma, Beauveria or Bt may be used only when the crop/pest/product use is supported and the label or expert guidance is followed.
♻️

How to make compost

  • Collect plant residues and suitable animal-manure materials; avoid plastics, diseased material and contaminated waste.
  • Build a moist, aerated pile with alternating carbon-rich dry material and nitrogen-rich green/manure material.
  • Turn the pile periodically to supply oxygen and maintain even decomposition.
  • Use mature, dark, crumbly compost rather than fresh manure directly beside sensitive roots.
💧

Water & mulch

  • Water according to crop stage, soil moisture and weather; avoid chronic over-irrigation.
  • Organic mulches can reduce evaporation, suppress weeds and moderate soil temperature.
  • Keep mulch away from the stem/collar when disease risk or excessive moisture is a concern.
🔬

Monitoring rule

  • Do not spray just because an insect is seen.
  • Identify the pest/disease and estimate its level before choosing a control.
  • Prefer integrated management and escalate uncertain cases to a KVK/agriculture officer.
On-farm preparations

Organic inputs farmers commonly use

These are knowledge cards, not a blanket prescription. Preparation recipes and rates can vary by system and source.

Compost / Vermicompost

Use mature material as an organic matter and nutrient source. Nutrient content varies, so do not assume a fixed N-P-K value without analysis.

Green manure

Grow a suitable green-manure crop and incorporate it at the appropriate stage for the local system. This can add organic matter and biologically supplied nitrogen.

Jeevamrit / Beejamrit / Ghanjeevamrit

These natural-farming inputs are used in some Indian natural-farming systems. ICAR extension programmes document their preparation and application; use a verified institutional recipe rather than a random social-media formula.

Neem-based inputs

Neem seed kernel extract, neem oil and other neem products are used in some crop IPM programmes. The correct concentration, crop and target pest must be checked against an institutional recommendation or product label.

Trichoderma & microbial biocontrols

Some ICAR crop programmes report Trichoderma and other microbial agents for disease management. Use the crop-specific, formulation-specific recommendation where available.

Biological pest control

Predators, parasitoids and microbial agents can suppress pests while protecting beneficial insects. Organic crop protection works best as a complete IPM programme, not as one magic spray.

Smart input recommendation

Which fertilizer or pesticide should I consider?

Select the crop, stage and observed problem. KhedutOS gives a source-backed starting recommendation where the official material supports it. It does not diagnose a pest from one symptom or invent a chemical dose.

Input Advisor

Safety: use only a product/formulation registered for the crop and target pest in India, follow the current label, PPE and waiting period. If the pest/disease is uncertain, contact a KVK or agriculture officer.

How the recommendation is built

🌱 Fertilizer: published crop/region nutrient baselines are used when available; soil-test results should override a generic baseline.
🐞 Pesticide: the app identifies a target pest from the selected problem and points to an officially registered active ingredient/use. Exact dose is not invented.
🔎 Before spraying: confirm the pest/disease, assess severity/threshold, protect beneficial insects and avoid unnecessary mixtures.
🛡️ PPQS / CIB&RC — registered pesticide-use references

Research-backed agronomy

Smart fertilizer & crop guidance

KhedutOS uses a curated knowledge base from official agricultural research and plant-protection sources. Exact recommendations are shown only within their published scope; otherwise the app asks for a soil test or expert confirmation.

Smart Fertilizer Advisor

Choose a crop and enter farm area. The engine returns a source-backed nutrient baseline when a published Gujarat recommendation is available. It does not invent a dose.

Important: N-P-K figures are nutrient quantities, not bags of urea/DAP/MOP. Product conversion depends on the fertilizer analysis and local recommendation.

Evidence & sources

The current knowledge base prioritizes ICAR, Anand Agricultural University, Junagadh Agricultural University and Government of India plant-protection material.

🌾 ICAR — national agricultural research and advisories
🌱 Anand Agricultural University — Gujarat crop research recommendations
🥜 Junagadh Agricultural University — Gujarat groundnut and crop recommendations
🛡️ PPQS / CIB&RC — Government of India plant-protection and registered pesticide-use material

No website can guarantee zero-error agronomy for every field. Soil, variety, season, irrigation, pest pressure and local recommendations can change the correct action.
